What you get

Privacy that is structural, not promised

Most apps ask you to trust their policy. Nyvox is built so there is very little to trust in the first place — the server simply never holds anything readable about you.

🆔

No phone, no email

Your identity is an Account ID starting with vc…, derived from a key pair generated on your device. There is no profile, no directory and no contact upload.

🔐

End-to-end encryption

An X25519 key exchange creates a shared secret between two devices, and every message is sealed with AES-256-GCM using a fresh random nonce. Plaintext never leaves your phone.

📝

12-word recovery phrase

A standard BIP-39 phrase is your entire account. Write it down once and restore the same identity on any device. Lose it and nobody — including us — can recover it.

⏱️

Disappearing messages

Set a per-chat timer: off, 10 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day or 1 week. Old messages clear themselves out instead of piling up in a database forever.

📷

Add contacts by ID or QR

Share your Account ID as text or scan a QR code in person. Your address book stays on your device and is never synced to a server.

⚡

Realtime and free

Messages arrive instantly over an encrypted realtime channel, on a backend that costs nothing to run — so the app has no reason to monetise you.

Under the hood

The server only ever sees ciphertext

Nyvox is a Flutter app talking to a locked-down Postgres backend. Anonymous sign-in is used purely as a transport credential so row-level security can decide what a device may touch — it is not an account and it holds no personal data.

Straight talk

What Nyvox protects — and what it doesn't

A privacy app that overstates itself is a security risk. Here is the honest split, so you can decide whether it fits your threat model.

Protected today

  • Message content is unreadable to the server, the database and anyone intercepting traffic.
  • No phone number, email, real name or contact list is ever collected.
  • Private keys stay in device secure storage and are never uploaded.
  • Database access is restricted by row-level security policies.
  • Disappearing timers limit how long anything lingers at all.

Not yet covered

  • No onion routing. The backend can see device IP addresses. Use a VPN or Tor if network-level metadata matters to you.
  • No forward secrecy yet. One long-lived key pair per identity; a double-ratchet upgrade is planned.
  • Groups are partial. The data model supports them, but group encryption and UI are still in progress.
  • Encrypted attachments and push notifications are on the roadmap, not shipped.

FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Do I really not need a phone number?

Correct. Nyvox never asks for a number, an email or a name. Your Account ID is generated mathematically from a key created on your device, so there is nothing linking the account to your real identity.

What happens if I lose my 12-word phrase?

Your identity is gone permanently. There is no reset link and no support recovery, because nobody else ever had your key. Store the phrase offline, ideally on paper in two separate places.

Can the Nyvox team read my messages?

No. Encryption and decryption happen on your device. The database stores only ciphertext and a random nonce for each message, which are useless without the shared secret held by the two devices in the conversation.

Is it open source?

Nyvox is developed by Zenvx Team on GitHub, and the backend schema and setup guide ship with the project so you can inspect exactly what is stored and run your own instance.

Why is the Android build an APK instead of a store listing?

Direct distribution keeps the install free of store accounts and analytics requirements. Each release is published with a checksum you can verify before installing.

Does it cost anything?

No. The backend runs comfortably on a free tier, so there is no subscription, no ads and no incentive to collect data about you.
